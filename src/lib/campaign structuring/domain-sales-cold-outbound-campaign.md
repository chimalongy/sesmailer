# Cold Outbound Campaign — Selling Domains (Afternic Portfolio)

Built for: domains purchased via registrars/drops, listed on Afternic, and sold directly to end-user buyers through proactive outreach (rather than waiting on marketplace inbound leads).

**Why outbound matters even with Afternic**: Afternic gets you inbound traffic from registrars in their network, but the highest-value buyers — a specific company that would actually use the domain — often never stumble onto a "for sale" landing page. Direct outreach to those buyers is usually what turns a $2,000 domain into a $15,000+ sale.

---

## Step 0: Finding the Right Prospects (before writing any email)

For each domain in your portfolio, identify 3–10 companies that are a strong brand/name match:

- **Exact match**: domain.com matches a company's exact name or product name
- **Close variant**: company uses a longer/hyphenated/misspelled version of your domain and would benefit from the cleaner version
- **Industry match**: domain is a strong generic term for a specific vertical (e.g., "cloudpay.com" → payment/fintech companies)
- **Rebrand signal**: companies that recently raised funding, rebranded, or are expanding into a new market/country often need a new domain

Good sources: Crunchbase, LinkedIn company search, Google ("[keyword] + company"), trademark databases (USPTO/TESS, EUIPO), and industry directories.

**Find the right contact**, not a generic inbox: Founder/CEO (small companies), CMO/Marketing Director (mid-size), Brand/Domain Portfolio Manager (larger companies — some have a dedicated person for this). Tools like Hunter.io, Apollo, or LinkedIn Sales Navigator help find verified emails.

## The Email Sequence

### Stage 1 — Day 0: The Opener
Keep this short. The only goal here is a reply, not a sale.

**Subject line options (test a few):**
- Quick question about [domain.com]
- [domain.com] — thought of [Company]
- Is [domain.com] relevant to [Company]?

**Body:**
> Hi [First Name],
>
> I own [domain.com] and it seemed like a strong match for [Company/Product]. Before doing anything else with it, I wanted to check whether it's of interest to your team.
>
> No pressure either way — just didn't want to offer it elsewhere without asking first.
>
> Worth a quick reply?
>
> [Your Name]

---

### Stage 2 — Day 4: Value & Reasoning
Only send if no reply. Add the "why" without pushing price yet.

**Subject:** Re: Quick question about [domain.com]

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Following up briefly. A few reasons this could be worth a look for [Company]:
>
> - Exact match to your brand name — helps with direct-navigation traffic and brand trust
> - Cleaner/shorter than your current domain (if applicable)
> - Keeps competitors or unrelated buyers from acquiring it later
>
> Happy to share what similar domains have sold for recently if useful context.
>
> [Your Name]

---

### Stage 3 — Day 9: Social Proof / Price Anchor
This is where you introduce real numbers — this is often the message that gets replies, because it removes ambiguity.

**Subject:** How other brands have priced domains like this

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Just to give you a sense of scale — exact-match domains in [industry] have sold in the [$X–$Y] range depending on brand fit and length of use. Not trying to push a number on you, just want you to have real context.
>
> If it's not a fit for [Company] right now, understood — I'll likely move it to public listing after this week.
>
> [Your Name]

---

### Stage 4 — Day 14: Soft Deadline / Final Nudge
This message consistently gets the best reply rates in domain sales — it gives a clean, low-pressure reason to respond now.

**Subject:** Closing this out — [domain.com]

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Wanted to close the loop — I'll be moving [domain.com] to active public listing this week unless there's interest from your side.
>
> If timing's just off, no worries — feel free to reach out down the line if things change.
>
> All the best,
> [Your Name]

---

### Stage 5 — Day 30+: Long-Tail Re-engagement (optional)
Only for domains that are high-value enough to keep warm. Send once, then stop.

**Subject:** Still available if it's useful later

> Hi [First Name],
>
> No response needed — just flagging that [domain.com] is still available if plans change on your end. This'll be my last note on it.
>
> [Your Name]

## Campaign Summary

| Stage | Day | Purpose | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Open the door, gauge interest | Curious, brief |
| 2 | 4 | Justify relevance/value | Informative |
| 3 | 9 | Price anchor via comps | Confident, factual |
| 4 | 14 | Urgency / soft deadline | Direct, polite |
| 5 | 30+ | Keep-warm re-engagement | Zero-pressure |

**Reply rate expectation:** Stage 1 and Stage 4 typically perform best — Stage 1 catches people who were already half-interested, Stage 4 catches people who needed a reason to stop deprioritizing it.

---

## Practical Notes for Your Setup

- **Sending domain**: Use a separate domain/inbox for outreach (not your primary flipping brand or your Afternic account email) to protect deliverability if a campaign gets flagged as spam.
- **Volume**: Personalize the company name, product, and industry comp numbers in every email — even at scale, a merge-tag personalized email meaningfully outperforms a fully generic one in this niche, since the recipient can tell if you actually looked at their brand.
- **When they reply "how much?"**: Have a fast, ready answer — a price range you're comfortable defending, not a single hard number, so there's room to negotiate. Slow responses lose deals in domain sales.
- **Redirect to Afternic if useful**: Once a prospect shows real interest, you can point them to the Afternic listing (or landing page) to handle the actual purchase/escrow flow, while you close the human negotiation over email.
- **Tracking**: Log domain, prospect, company, stage sent, and reply status in a spreadsheet so follow-ups don't get missed or duplicated across your portfolio.
- **Compliance**: Include a simple opt-out line ("let me know if you'd prefer not to hear from me on this") — helps replies and keeps you clear of spam complaints at any real volume.
