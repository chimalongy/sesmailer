# Cold Outbound Campaign — Selling Geo Domains

For domains like [City][Niche].com, [City]Homes.com, [Region][Service].com — where the value comes from local relevance and SEO/direct-navigation potential rather than a single exact brand match.

**Why geo domains sell differently:** There's rarely one "obvious" buyer like there is with a brand-match domain. Instead, there's usually a whole category of local businesses who'd benefit — meaning your prospect list is bigger, but each individual pitch needs to lean harder on local SEO value and lead-gen potential, not brand identity.

---

## Step 0: Building the Prospect List

For a domain like **AustinPlumbers.com**, your buyer pool typically includes:

- **Local service businesses** in that niche (plumbers, realtors, dentists, movers, etc. based in that city) — especially ones currently ranking on page 2–3 of Google, since a geo-match domain could meaningfully help them
- **Franchise operators / regional franchisees** — a franchisee for that specific city often has a marketing budget and a direct incentive to own the geo-match domain for their territory
- **Local business associations / chambers of commerce** (less common buyer, but relevant for city-wide generic terms like VisitAustin-style domains)
- **Multi-location companies** expanding into that market who want a localized landing domain
- **Local real estate teams/brokerages** for realty-flavored geo domains
- **Marketing agencies serving that city/niche** — sometimes buy geo domains for clients or to run their own local lead-gen sites

**Where to find them:**
- Google search "[niche] + [city]" and note who ranks on page 1–3 (page 2–3 businesses are often the best targets — motivated to improve rank)
- Google Maps / Yelp listings for that niche + city
- Franchise directories (e.g., look up "[brand] franchise [city]" to find the specific franchisee)
- LinkedIn: search local business owners/marketing managers in that city + industry

## The Email Sequence

### Stage 1 — Day 0: The Opener
Lead with the SEO/local relevance angle, not just "I have a domain for sale."

**Subject line options:**
- Domain for [City] [niche] businesses
- Question about [domain.com]
- Thought this might help your [City] rankings

**Body:**
> Hi [First Name],
>
> I own [domain.com] and thought it could be a strong fit for a [City]-based [niche] business like yours — exact-match local domains like this tend to help with both direct-navigation traffic and local search relevance.
>
> Wanted to check if it's something you'd have interest in before offering it elsewhere.
>
> Worth a quick reply?
>
> [Your Name]

---

### Stage 2 — Day 4: Local SEO / Lead-Gen Value
This is the stage where geo domains differ most from brand-match domains — the pitch is about traffic and rankings, not identity.

**Subject:** Re: Domain for [City] [niche] businesses

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Following up briefly. A few reasons a domain like this tends to help local businesses specifically:
>
> - Exact-match city + service names still carry meaningful local SEO weight
> - Easy for customers to remember and type directly (reduces reliance on ads)
> - Can be pointed to a dedicated landing page to capture city-specific leads separately from your main site
>
> Happy to share what similar city-niche domains have gone for if that's useful context.
>
> [Your Name]

---

### Stage 3 — Day 9: Comps / Price Anchor
Geo domain pricing varies a lot by city size and niche competitiveness — anchor with a realistic range for their specific market.

**Subject:** What domains like this typically go for

> Hi [First Name],
>
> For context — geo-match domains in mid-size markets for [niche] have generally sold in the [$X–$Y] range, with larger metro areas trending higher. Not pushing a number on you, just want you to have a realistic sense before deciding.
>
> If it's not the right time, understood — I'll likely list it more broadly after this week, which usually opens it up to competitors in the space too.
>
> [Your Name]

---

### Stage 4 — Day 14: Soft Deadline
Same structure as brand-domain campaigns — this stage usually gets the best reply rate.

**Subject:** Closing this out — [domain.com]

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Wanted to close the loop on this — I'll be moving [domain.com] to a broader listing this week unless there's interest on your end.
>
> If timing's just off, no worries — happy to reconnect down the line if things change.
>
> All the best,
> [Your Name]

---

### Stage 5 — Day 30+: Long-Tail Re-engagement (optional)
Worth doing for higher-value metro/niche combos where you have a short prospect list and want to keep the door open.

**Subject:** Still available if useful later

> Hi [First Name],
>
> No response needed — just flagging [domain.com] is still available if plans change. This'll be my last note on it.
>
> [Your Name]

## Campaign Summary

| Stage | Day | Purpose | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Open the door, local relevance hook | Curious, brief |
| 2 | 4 | SEO / lead-gen value explanation | Informative |
| 3 | 9 | Market-specific price anchor | Confident, factual |
| 4 | 14 | Urgency / soft deadline | Direct, polite |
| 5 | 30+ | Keep-warm re-engagement | Zero-pressure |

## Practical Notes for Geo Domains

- **List size vs. brand-match domains**: You'll usually have far more viable prospects per domain (every plumber in a city, not one company) — this makes geo domains a better fit for higher-volume, moderately personalized outreach rather than deep 1:1 personalization on every email.
- **Franchise angle is often underused**: For franchise-heavy niches (real estate, fitness, home services), reaching out to the specific franchisee for that city — not corporate — usually gets better results, since they control local marketing spend directly.
- **Multi-location businesses expanding into the city** are worth watching for — a company opening a new location in that market has an immediate, time-sensitive reason to want the geo-match domain, which shortens your sales cycle.
- **Landing page helps conversion**: For geo domains, having a simple "domain available" landing page with the price/contact info (even if not fully public-listed) gives prospects something to visit if they want more proof it's real before replying to a cold email.
- **Repeat buyers**: Agencies and franchise groups that buy one geo domain from you often need others in different cities — worth tracking anyone who replies positively even if that specific deal doesn't close, since they're a warm lead for other domains in your portfolio.
- **Same deliverability/compliance basics apply**: separate sending domain, opt-out line, and a spreadsheet tracker per domain-city-niche combo, since your total contact volume will likely be higher here than with brand-match campaigns.
