# Cold Outbound Campaign — Selling EMDs (Exact Match Domains, No Geo)

For domains like **WeBuyHouses.com, SellMyCar.com, PetInsurance.com** — where the value comes from category ownership, nationwide type-in traffic, and brand-ability, rather than local relevance.

**Why EMDs sell differently than geo domains:** With a geo domain, there are dozens of local businesses who could each benefit a little. With a national EMD, there's usually a small pool of companies who could benefit *a lot* — often just the category leaders, well-funded challengers, or PE-backed roll-ups in that space. Fewer prospects, but each one is higher-intent and the deal sizes are typically much larger, since the domain functions as a category asset (a mini-brand in itself) rather than a local lead-gen page.

---

## Step 0: Building the Prospect List

For a domain like **WeBuyHouses.com**, your buyer pool typically includes:

- **National/regional players in that exact service or product category** — companies already doing this business under a weaker or longer domain name who'd upgrade for the direct-navigation traffic and credibility
- **Franchisors (the corporate parent, not a local franchisee)** — since the domain isn't tied to one territory, it's the franchisor who has the incentive (a nationwide brand asset benefits every territory, not just one)
- **PE-backed roll-ups / industry consolidators** — private equity groups buying up companies in a fragmented category (home services, insurance agencies, etc.) often want a category-defining domain to unify their portfolio under
- **Well-funded challengers or new entrants** — startups raising a round and needing a credible, memorable domain to compete against an incumbent
- **Affiliate marketers / lead-gen arbitrage operators** — buy the EMD, build a comparison/lead-gen site, and sell leads to companies in that space rather than operating the underlying business themselves
- **The current category leader** — sometimes the biggest player in the space is the best buyer simply to keep the domain out of a competitor's hands (defensive purchase)

**Where to find them:**
- Google search the exact keyword/category nationally and see who's running paid ads or ranking organically — tools like SEMrush or SpyFu show who's spending heavily on that keyword, which signals budget and motivation
- Trade publications and industry association member directories for that category
- Franchise Disclosure Documents (FDDs) and franchise directories — search for the *franchisor*, not a specific location
- Crunchbase/PitchBook for recently-funded startups in that category (fresh funding = budget + rebrand motivation)
- LinkedIn: search VP Marketing / CMO / Growth at companies in that category
- Trademark databases (USPTO TESS) for registered brand names close to the domain's keyword — signals who's already invested in owning that category identity

## The Email Sequence

### Stage 1 — Day 0: The Opener
Lead with category ownership and direct-navigation traffic, not "I have a domain for sale."

**Subject line options:**
- Question about [domain.com]
- [domain.com] — thought of you
- Category domain for [industry/service]

**Body:**
> Hi,
>
> I own [domain.com] and thought it could be a strong fit given what you're building in [category] — exact-match domains like this tend to carry meaningful direct-navigation traffic (people just typing it in) and instant category recognition.
>
> Wanted to check if it's something you'd have interest in before offering it elsewhere.
>
> Worth a quick reply?
>
> [Your Name]

---

### Stage 2 — Day 4: Category Ownership / Traffic Value
This is where EMD pitches differ most from geo pitches — the angle is brand equity and type-in traffic, not local search rank.

**Subject:** Re: [domain.com]

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Following up briefly. A few reasons domains like this tend to matter more as a company scales:
>
> - Exact-match category domains still carry real type-in and referral traffic, independent of any ad spend
> - Instantly communicates what you do — no explaining the brand name
> - Becomes a durable asset that compounds in value as the category grows, unlike a domain tied to a sub-brand or campaign name
>
> Happy to share what comparable category-defining domains have sold for if useful.
>
> [Your Name]

---

### Stage 3 — Day 9: Comps / Price Anchor
EMD pricing varies enormously by category size, competitiveness, and monetization potential (insurance, finance, and legal EMDs command far more than niche consumer categories) — anchor with real comps, not a geo-style range.

**Subject:** What domains like this typically go for

> Hi [First Name],
>
> For context — exact-match category domains in [industry] have generally traded in the [$X–$Y] range, with premium categories (finance, insurance, legal, high-CPC verticals) trending well above that. Not pushing a number on you, just want you to have a realistic sense before deciding.
>
> If it's not the right time, understood — I'll likely list it more broadly after this week, which usually brings in interest from direct competitors too.
>
> [Your Name]

---

### Stage 4 — Day 14: Soft Deadline
Same structure as other campaigns — this stage usually gets the best reply rate, especially once a competitor becomes a plausible alternate buyer.

**Subject:** Closing this out — [domain.com]

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Wanted to close the loop on this — I'll be moving [domain.com] to a broader listing this week unless there's interest on your end.
>
> If timing's just off, no worries — happy to reconnect down the line if things change.
>
> All the best,
> [Your Name]

---

### Stage 5 — Day 30+: Long-Tail Re-engagement (optional)
Worth doing for high-value categories (finance, insurance, legal, home services) where the prospect list is short and each contact is worth staying warm with.

**Subject:** Still available if useful later

> Hi [First Name],
>
> No response needed — just flagging [domain.com] is still available if plans change. This'll be my last note on it.
>
> [Your Name]

## Practical Notes for EMDs (No Geo)

- **List size vs. geo domains**: Your prospect list will be much shorter — often just a handful of real candidates per domain — since there's usually one category, not one-per-city. This makes EMDs a better fit for deep 1:1 personalization on every email rather than volume outreach.
- **Franchisor, not franchisee**: Since the domain has no territory attached, the buyer is the corporate/franchisor level — they're the one who benefits from a nationwide asset, not an individual location owner.
- **Funding events are a strong trigger**: A company that just raised a round or just got acquired/rolled up by PE is often in-market for a stronger brand domain — worth tracking funding news in the category and reaching out shortly after.
- **Price ceiling is category-driven, not size-driven**: Unlike geo domains (where metro size drives price), EMD value is driven by the category's CPC/LTV — insurance, legal, finance, and B2B SaaS categories can support far higher prices than consumer lifestyle categories, even with similar search volume.
- **Defensive buyers exist**: The current market leader may buy purely to deny a competitor the domain — this is worth mentioning subtly (e.g., "before offering it elsewhere") without naming competitors directly, since it's a real (if soft) pressure point.
- **Landing page still helps**: A simple "domain available" page with traffic/valuation context gives prospects something to check before replying, same as with geo domains — but for EMDs it's worth including any existing type-in traffic or backlink stats if you have them, since that's the core value prop.
- **Repeat buyers are rarer but bigger**: A PE roll-up or franchisor who buys one category EMD from you may want others in adjacent categories, but this is a smaller, longer-cycle relationship than the "same buyer, different city" pattern in geo domains — treat these as high-touch relationships, not warm-lead lists.
- **Same deliverability/compliance basics apply**: separate sending domain, opt-out line, and a spreadsheet tracker per domain — but expect fewer total contacts per domain and a longer average sales cycle than geo campaigns.
